Three ways to put you at the helm

One boat in one harbor, drawn three ways at once. Steer with ← →, go ahead with ↑ and astern with ↓ (or WASD, or the buttons below). Every view follows the same boat, so you can compare how hard it is to judge the slip from each one.

1

Fake 3-D in Phaser

Effort: small
Tilted 2-D chart · Phaser can do this
Good
Stays inside ADR 0001. No new library, and the PR's harbor art mostly carries over.
Catch
Everything is painted on the water. Piers, pilings and boats have no height, so you can't really judge how close you are.
Needs
Nothing new approved.
2

Three.js helm view

Effort: medium
Real 3-D in the browser
Good
Real depth: pilings, hulls and the pier edge stand up. Runs at /play, offline, on the PR's existing src/sim.
Catch
From the helm you can't see your own sides or stern. It needs the Helm/Chase toggle below.
Needs
An ADR 0001 change that Et approves.
3

Unity

Effort: large
Stand-in for Unity-level detail
Good
The best water, lighting and boat motion, and a path to native apps.
Catch
Large download in a browser, a separate toolchain, and golden replays would need redoing in C#.
Needs
A Sweet Potato ADR and a Unity lane.

Unity can't run inside this page. This view is Three.js with waves, light reflections and hull motion added, to suggest the extra fidelity.

What carries over from PR #26

The PR keeps the boat physics in src/sim with no Phaser or drawing code. Options 1 and 2 only replace the drawing. The sim, the mission JSON and both golden replays stay as they are. Option 3 starts the simulator over in C#.

What this mockup is not

  • The physics here is a quick stand-in, not the PR's integer sim.
  • The harbor is generic, not the real Long Beach layout.
  • Effort tags are my estimates, not measured.

My pick

Option 2, with a Helm/Chase toggle and the gold arrow kept in 3-D. It's real depth for a fraction of Unity's cost, and it doesn't rule Unity out later.
